# Pricing and Engagement Models

## Overview

This document outlines pricing models, engagement structures, and guidance for transitioning between consulting and B2B contract work.

**Important**: All pricing is indicative. Final pricing depends on scope, complexity, urgency, client type, and delivery responsibility. This document does not constitute legal, tax, or financial advice.

---

## Pricing Models

### 1. Hourly / Day Rate

| Rate Type                 | Range (EUR)     | Range (PLN)       | Notes                                |
| ------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Standard day rate**     | €600 – €1,000   | 2,600 – 4,300 PLN | For ongoing work, extensions         |
| **Senior/strategic rate** | €800 – €1,200   | 3,500 – 5,200 PLN | For architecture, leadership work    |
| **Emergency rate**        | +20–30% premium | —                 | For urgent / weekend / critical work |

**When to use**:

- Scope is unclear or evolving
- Client prefers time-based billing
- Extension of existing fixed-scope work
- Flexible fractional arrangements

**Pros**:

- Simple to track and invoice
- Flexible for evolving scope
- Easy to explain

**Cons**:

- Rewards hours, not outcomes
- Can feel expensive for small asks
- Less predictable for client budgeting

---

### 2. Fixed-Price Audit

| Package                           | Range (EUR)      | Range (PLN)         | Notes                         |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Architecture & Strategy Audit** | €4,000 – €10,000 | 17,000 – 43,000 PLN | 1–2 weeks, fixed deliverables |
| **Quick assessment**              | €2,000 – €4,000  | 8,500 – 17,000 PLN  | 2–3 days, limited scope       |

**When to use**:

- Clear entry point for new clients
- Client wants budget predictability
- Scope can be defined upfront
- Discovery before larger engagement

**Pros**:

- Easy for client to approve
- Clear deliverables and timeline
- Rewards efficiency
- Good entry point offer

**Cons**:

- Risk if scope expands
- Need clear scope definition
- May need change request process

**Recommendation**: Use fixed-price Audit as primary entry offer.

---

### 3. Fixed-Scope PoC / MVP

| Package                | Range (EUR)       | Range (PLN)          | Notes                    |
| ---------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **PoC / MVP Delivery** | €10,000 – €30,000 | 43,000 – 130,000 PLN | 3–6 weeks, defined scope |

**When to use**:

- After Audit, scope is clear
- Time-boxed delivery with defined outcomes
- Client needs proof of concept

**Pros**:

- Clear deliverables
- Budget predictability
- Demonstrates value quickly

**Cons**:

- Scope must be well-defined
- Risk if requirements change
- May need change request for additions

**Recommendation**: Offer after successful Audit when scope is clear.

### 4. Monthly Retainer

| Commitment        | Range (EUR/month) | Range (PLN/month)   | Notes                      |
| ----------------- | ----------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **1–2 days/week** | €4,000 – €10,000  | 17,000 – 43,000 PLN | Fractional leadership      |
| **2–3 days/week** | €8,000 – €15,000  | 35,000 – 65,000 PLN | More intensive involvement |
| **4–5 days/week** | €12,000 – €20,000 | 52,000 – 87,000 PLN | Near full-time embedded    |

**When to use**:

- Fractional Technical Data Lead engagements
- Ongoing platform support
- Post-launch stabilization
- Advisory / architecture oversight

**Pros**:

- Predictable income
- Long-term relationship
- Flexible scope within commitment
- Client locks in availability

**Cons**:

- Capacity commitment
- May conflict with other work
- Need to manage scope creep

**Recommendation**: Good for Fractional Technical Data Lead and post-Launch support.

### 5. Partner / Subcontractor Model

| Model                                | Typical Margin          | Notes                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Subcontractor for software house** | 70–85% of client rate   | Partner takes project risk, handles invoicing |
| **Partner referral**                 | 10–20% referral fee     | Or reciprocal referrals                       |
| **Joint delivery**                   | Revenue share by effort | Both parties deliver                          |

**When to use**:

- Working through partner channel
- Project comes from software house or consultancy
- Don't want to handle client relationship directly

**Pros**:

- Access to larger projects
- Reduced sales effort
- Partner handles some risk
- Network building

**Cons**:

- Lower margin
- Less direct client relationship
- Dependent on partner pipeline

**Recommendation**: Good for building partner channel alongside direct work.

### 6. Classic B2B Contract

| Contract Type                | Typical Range (EUR/month) | Notes                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Lead Data Engineer**       | €8,000 – €15,000          | Full-time equivalent |
| **Technical Data Lead**      | €10,000 – €18,000         | Leadership role      |

**When to use**:

- Benefits from seniuts credibility
- Can build case material

**Cons**:

- Less flexibility
- May conflict with consulting work
- Positioning as employee vs consultant

**Recommendation**: Use for income stability while building consulting pipeline.

## Avoiding Common Pricing Mistakes

### 1. Underpricing Strategic Work

**Problem**: Charging hourly for architecture work that delivers €50,000+ of value.

**Solution**: Use fixed-price Audit. Price based on value delivered, not hours spent.

### 2. Mixing Discovery and Implementation

**Problem**: Doing unpaid discovery hoping for implementation contract.

**Solution**: Always charge for discovery (Audit). Implementation is separate decision.

### 3. Scope Creep Without Change Requests

**Problem**: Fixed-price engagement grows beyond original scope.

**Solution**: Define scope clearly. Document what's included and excluded. Use change requests.

### 4. Competing on Price

**Problem**: Lowering rates to win work against cheaper competitors.

**Solution**: Compete on experience, quality, and outcomes. Walk away from price-sensitive buyers.

### 5. Free Proposals

**Problem**: Writing detailed proposals for prospects who don't convert.

**Solution**: Limit unpaid proposal work. Use paid Audit for detailed discovery.

## Separating Discovery from Implementation

| Phase                  | Paid? | Deliverable                            | Decision Point      |
| ---------------------- | ----- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Initial call**       | No    | Qualification, fit assessment          | Proceed to Audit?   |
| **Architecture Audit** | Yes   | Architecture, roadmap, recommendations | Proceed to PoC/MVP? |
| **PoC / MVP**          | Yes   | Working foundation, proof              | Proceed to Launch?  |
| **Launch & Transfer**  | Yes   | Production platform, handover          | Retainer or end?    |

**Key principle**: Each phase is paid. Each phase ends with a clear decision point. Client can stop after any phase with value delivered.

## Transitioning from B2B Contract to Consulting

### While in B2B Contract

1. Build seniuts presence (website, LinkedIn, one-pager)
2. Maintain professional visibility
3. Collect experience and case material
4. Network with potential clients and partners
5. Take consulting calls in off-hours if feasible

### Between Contracts

1. Prioritize consulting pipeline
2. Reach out to network and partners
3. Pursue Architecture Audit opportunities
4. Accept B2B contract if needed for income
5. Balance short-term income with long-term positioning

### Transitioning

1. Start with Fractional / part-time consulting while in contract
2. Build pipeline before leaving contract
3. Have 3–6 months runway or backup income
4. Don't announce "full-time consulting" until pipeline supports it

## Rate Negotiation Guidance

| Situation                             | Approach                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Client asks for discount              | Reduce scope, not rate                       |
| Rate seems too high for client        | Offer smaller Audit as entry                 |
| Client wants hourly, you prefer fixed | Explain fixed-scope benefits, offer choice   |
| Emergency / urgent work               | Apply premium (20–30%)                       |
| Long-term retainer                    | Small discount (5–10%) for commitment        |
| Partner / subcontractor               | Accept lower margin for reduced sales effort |

---

## Invoicing Basics

- Invoice monthly or per milestone
- Net 14–30 payment terms typical
- Require deposit (30–50%) for larger fixed-scope work
- B2B invoicing (no VAT between EU businesses with VAT numbers)
- Track all invoices and payment status
- Follow up promptly on overdue payments

**Note**: Consult an accountant for tax, VAT, and invoicing specifics in Poland.
